Energyline Dashboard

Helping teams identify what needs attention across complex workflows

Tags:

Product Designer

UI

Design Systems

Project

Energyline is a product concept exploring a more focused way for internal teams to manage energy-installation applications, review information, and identify next steps.

Goals

  • Simplify navigation across complex application information.
  • Surface actionable insights about applications and their progress.
  • Explore AI assistance to make application reviews faster and easier.

Making complex workflows easier to navigate

Fragmented information makes it harder to review applications, track progress, and identify what needs attention.

The goal was to help teams:

Identify priorities without searching across multiple views.
Understand application status and find relevant information quickly.
Move to the next action without unnecessary navigation.

Designing around visibility and action

Rather than adding more information to the interface, the concept focuses on making existing information easier to understand and act on.

Prioritize what matters.

Bring key metrics, application statuses, and items requiring attention into a clear hierarchy.

Connect overview to detail.

Let users move from a high-level view to the information needed to review an application.

Support informed decisions.

Explore AI-assisted summaries that help users understand application details while keeping the original information accessible.

Balancing visibility with action

The dashboard brings key metrics, application statuses, and relevant actions into one workspace, helping teams understand workload and identify what needs attention.

Connecting status to the next action

The application view groups customer information, status, and supporting details to help reviewers understand each case and determine the next step.

AI as a starting point, not the decision maker

The AI assistant explores how concise summaries can help users understand applications faster. Original information remains accessible for verification, and decisions stay with the user.

Incomplete or conflicting data would need clear handling to avoid misleading summaries.

Building a consistent interface

Semantic tokens and reusable components help maintain consistency across the interface. The structure supports light and dark themes while keeping visual hierarchy and accessibility in focus.

Design decisions and trade-offs

01.

Prioritizing actionable information

I prioritized application status, key metrics, and items requiring attention over a purely informational dashboard.

Trade-off: More information remains visible, but the hierarchy needs to prevent visual overload.

02.

Separating overview from detail

I split workload monitoring from application review, letting users move from a high-level overview to focused case information.

Trade-off: A clearer hierarchy requires navigation between views, so key context must remain easy to find.

03.

Using AI to support review

I explored AI-generated summaries to help users understand application details without replacing the underlying data.

Trade-off: Summaries can reduce reading effort, but introduce risks around missing context and accuracy. Users need access to source information and remain responsible for decisions.
