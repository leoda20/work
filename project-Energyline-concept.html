Energyline Dashboard

Energyline is a dashboard redesign focused way to improve the internal teams management in energy-installation applications, review information, and identify next steps.

Role:

Product Designer

Tags:

Product Design

UI

Design Systems

The challenge

Making complex workflows easier to navigate

Fragmented information combined with multiple layers of information makes it harder to review applications, track progress, and identify what needs attention.

The goal was to help teams:

01

Identify priorities without searching across multiple views.

02

Understand application status and find relevant information quickly.

03

Overview the progress and status of applications at a glance.

The dashboard

Designing around visibility and action

Rather than adding more information to the interface, the idea was to focus on making existing information easier to understand and act on. Adding more visual appealing ways to present information.

To move towards that goal, the objectives were:

Prioritize what matters.

Bring key metrics, application statuses, and items requiring attention into a clear hierarchy.

Connect overview to detail.

Let users move from a high-level view to the information needed to review an application.

Support informed decisions.

Explore AI-assisted summaries that help users understand application details keeping the information accessible.

The application view

Balancing visibility with action

While the dashboard brings key metrics, application statuses, and relevant actions into one workspace, The application view is meant to provide a more detailed look at each application in block, generating a sumary with the essential and more important information.

Implementing AI

AI as a starting point, not the decision maker

Including an AI assistant was necessary to explores and generate concise summaries to help users understand applications faster. Original information remains accessible for verification, and decisions stay available to the user.

Incomplete, pending or conflicting data would need clear handling to avoid misleading summaries.

Theme modes

Building a consistent interface

Applying semantic tokens previously implemented and reusable components helped maintain consistency across the interface. The structure was built to support light and dark themes while keeping visual hierarchy and accessibility in focus.

In summary

Design decisions and trade-offs

01.

Prioritizing actionable information

I prioritized application status, key metrics, and items requiring attention over a purely informational dashboard.

Trade-off:

More information remains visible, but the hierarchy needs to prevent visual overload.

02.

Separating overview from detail

I split workload monitoring from application review, letting users move from a high-level overview to focused case information.

Trade-off:

A clearer hierarchy requires navigation between views, so key context must remain easy to find.

03.

Using AI to support review

I explored AI-generated summaries to help users understand application details without replacing the underlying data.

Trade-off:

Summaries can reduce reading effort, but introduce risks around missing context and accuracy. Users need access to source information and remain responsible for decisions.

Outcome

From fragmented workflows to unified experience

The redesigned dashboard and application views bring key metrics, progress, and actions together. Clearer hierarchy and AI-powered summaries help teams scan information, understand application status, and act with greater confidence.
