Accountability Blueprint

A service blueprint mapping the Accountability Team's end-to-end workflow across customer interactions, internal operations, and supporting systems.

Role:

Service Designer

Tags:

Documentation

UX Strategy

Optimization

Overview

The Accountability Team relied on a complex workflow involving multiple systems, manual processes, and cross-functional teams.

As the product evolved, delays, duplicated work, and usability issues became increasingly difficult to trace.

I brought stakeholders together to map the service end to end, uncover operational friction, and create a shared foundation for future improvements.

The challenge

Complexity hidden across teams

Issues reported by one team often originated elsewhere in the workflow. Without a shared view of the service, understanding dependencies and identifying improvement opportunities was difficult.

The challenge: How might we make the entire service visible and help teams understand where friction originates?

Mapping the service

From isolated perspectives to a shared view

I worked with stakeholders across the product to document the service through three layers. Together, these layers connected the customer experience with the operations behind it.

Customer Journey

Mapped user actions, goals, decisions, and interactions across the service.

Frontstage

Documented customer-facing interfaces, notifications, support interactions, and system feedback.

Backstage

Connected internal processes, teams, and systems supporting each interaction — revealing dependencies and operational bottlenecks.

The complete service blueprint

The complete blueprint brought customer interactions, internal workflows, and operational dependencies into one view — helping teams understand how different parts of the service connected.

The Process

Getting everyone aligned

The main challenge wasn't documenting the workflow. It was helping stakeholders understand the purpose and value of the blueprint.

Each session involved explaining the approach, gathering details from different teams, and connecting individual perspectives into one coherent service.

I consolidated the findings into an initial blueprint, then brought stakeholders together again to validate missing dependencies and align the complete workflow.

Finding friction

Connecting problems to their root causes

Once the service was mapped, feedback and pain points became easier to connect across teams.

Five recurring friction areas emerged:

  • Repeated manual work
  • Unnecessary approvals
  • Unclear ownership
  • Disconnected systems
  • Inconsistent communication

The blueprint helped distinguish isolated usability issues from problems rooted in operational dependencies.

Prioritizing improvements

From visibility to action

With the main friction points identified, stakeholders worked together to define improvement opportunities.

The blueprint connected these opportunities to customer impact and operational constraints.

Automation

Reduce repetitive manual tasks.

Visibility

Improve access to system and process information.

Communication

Reduce unnecessary touchpoints and introduce contextual guidance.

Simplification

Streamline critical workflows and approvals.

Design opportunity

Connecting user friction to improvement opportunities

Recent user research findings were added to the service blueprint to validate and support the friction points identified during the mapping process. The research highlighted difficulties users faced when searching for account types and completing required configuration steps, reinforcing the need for clearer guidance throughout the experience.

1. Difficulty finding account types

Users struggled to identify and search for the correct account nature.

2. Incomplete setup

Some users did not complete the configuration process due to difficulties during registration.

3. Confusing account type selection

The account nature screen was confusing, making it harder for users to understand available options.

Guide users through the required information

Make each step clearer, explain what information is needed, and guide users through completing the required fields — reducing uncertainty during registration and configuration.

Creating a shared understanding

One service. Multiple perspectives.

The blueprint became a communication tool for Product, Engineering, Business, and the Accountability Team.

Instead of discussing isolated screens or individual complaints, stakeholders could understand how decisions affected the wider service.

Before guiding future improvements, the blueprint was reviewed with senior stakeholders and end users to validate its accuracy.

Outcome

Making complex services visible

The blueprint connected customer experience, internal operations, and system dependencies into one shared reference.

Better visibility

Teams understood the complete service ecosystem.

Faster prioritization

Pain points became easier to identify and discuss.

Cross-functional alignment

Stakeholders shared a common decision-making tool.

Foundation for future improvements

The blueprint supported future discovery and workflow optimization.

What I learned

Service design isn't only about documenting processes. Its value comes from helping people connect perspectives, understand dependencies, and make better decisions together.

The most important work was translating fragmented knowledge into a shared understanding that teams could use beyond the mapping exercise.
